Title: The Age of Exploration: Why Finance AI Is Fast and Cash Is Still Slow | Future CFO_DP 17 Sep 2026
Description:
On 16 September 2026 Auditoria.AI released its seventh annual State of AI Automation in the Finance Office report, “The Age of Exploration.” Two-thirds of finance organisations say they are increasing AI spend. Only about one in five report meaningful, measurable results. Average teams now use AI across 2.43 functions — a 36% jump in a year — yet most remain in explore-or-pilot mode. Invoice digitisation and error detection are common. Cash-cycle transformation is not.

Same week: OpenAI and PwC announced a collaboration to build finance agents around planning, close, procurement and treasury — with a live procurement agent inside OpenAI’s own finance organisation. Deloitte separately found 60% of finance leaders expect AI cost and complexity to rise substantially through 2027, and 54% say finance now leads enterprise AI capital allocation.

This episode is a teaching case for BCom, MCom, MBA and CA students: speed is not the same as cash conversion. Agents can process invoices. They cannot, by themselves, rewrite supplier terms, collection discipline, or the human who still signs.

Sources: Auditoria.AI / GlobeNewswire 16 Sep 2026; OpenAI–PwC announcement; CFO Dive / Deloitte AI-cost survey.

Auditoria.AI’s 16 September 2026 report calls this “The Age of Exploration.”
Investment is decisive. Maturity is not. 58.4% of teams are still exploring or piloting. Only 12.8% have reached optimising or autonomous operations. Deployment breadth rose 36% in one year to 2.43 functions per team. Digitisation and anomaly detection lead. Cash-cycle outcomes lag.

OpenAI and PwC are now building agents on live finance workflows — planning, reporting, procurement, payments, treasury, tax, close. Deloitte finds 60% of finance leaders expect AI cost and complexity to keep rising through 2027, and more than half already lead enterprise AI capital allocation.

For students: do not confuse a faster invoice scan with a shorter cash conversion cycle. The first is a task. The second is a system — terms, collections, inventory, and a human who still decides.
