Post:
Deloitte’s latest finance survey, reported by CFO Dive on 15 September 2026, is not a technology story. It is a cost-accounting story.

Sixty percent of finance leaders expect AI costs and technical complexity to rise substantially through 2027. Only 35 percent expect the rise to stay modest and plan to keep current practices. Fifty-four percent of finance leaders now lead enterprise AI and technology capital-allocation decisions. Forty-eight percent oversee the spending and the cost controls.

Three frictions sit underneath those numbers:
1. Regulatory and compliance uncertainty.
2. Cloud and vendor billing for AI compute that does not look like a traditional software licence.
3. AI usage data that does not join cleanly to ERP and finance systems.

On 16 September, Reuters published Oxford visiting professor Robert Eccles on a related problem: mapping sustainability risks to specific line items in the income statement, cash-flow statement and balance sheet. With Columbia’s Shivaram Rajgopal, he used AI to cut an expert’s mapping work from about 100 hours to about 30. His organisational answer is blunt: the CSO should report to the CFO.

Same week, ISSB Chair Emmanuel Faber noted 46 countries have decided to adopt ISSB standards, with a new ISSB seat planned in Geneva.

The student takeaway is not “learn more prompts.” It is: design a cost object for AI, a control for agent spend, and a mapping from climate risk to cash.
