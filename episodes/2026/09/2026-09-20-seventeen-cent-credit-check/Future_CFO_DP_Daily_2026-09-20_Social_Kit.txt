Title:
The 17-Cent Credit Check: Who Signs the AI Number? | Future CFO_DP
Description:
On 16 September 2026 Business Insider reported OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar telling CNBC's Jim Cramer that finance used the firm's own products to cut vendor credit checks from about $200 each to 17 cents, on a prior-year run-rate of 2,800 checks. She called many of the displaced tasks mundane. That is a real unit-cost story. It is not a control story.

Two other public facts land in the same week. Deloitte's survey of 1,434 finance leaders at companies with at least $1 billion revenue across 26 countries (CFO Dive, 15 September 2026) found 60% expect AI costs and technical complexity to rise substantially through 2027. Fifty-four percent said finance leads enterprise AI and technology capital allocation; 48% oversee AI and technology spend. Ed Hardy of Deloitte put it plainly: the CFO mandate is expanding from stewardship to how the firm invests in, governs and creates value from AI.

At Workiva Amplify in Las Vegas on 14 September 2026, Workiva CFO Barbara Larson said finance is finally in the room before the decision is made — and is being asked to lead strategy and tech strategy. Chief Customer Officer Penny Ashley-Lawrence cited survey data that 84% of respondents felt at least somewhat confident that AI-generated numbers could land in an annual report without a human ever checking them.

This Future CFO_DP Daily case does not invent OpenAI's books. It uses a labelled composite — Kalinga Precision Components Ltd and CFO Nandini Rao — to force a board choice: scale agentic credit and invoice work for unit-cost wins; freeze agents on any number that can reach statutory filings; dual-run human review until COSO-style evidence exists; or cap AI opex until FY2027 cost curves are visible.
