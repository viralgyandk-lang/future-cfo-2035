On 21 September 2026, CFO.com reported Deloitte’s Finance Trends 2027 finding: 60% of 1,434 finance leaders expect AI costs and complexity to rise substantially through 2027. Only 35% think the bill stays modest. 54% of finance leaders now lead AI capital allocation.

This episode is an HBS-style case for business students: how a mid-market CFO designs AI cost management before budget lock — without confusing a chatbot invoice with a capital programme.

LINKEDIN
Hook: 60% of CFOs say the AI bill keeps rising through 2027. 35% say it stays modest. Both groups sit in the same survey. Which camp is your finance team in — and can you prove it with a cost ledger, not a slide?
Post: Deloitte asked 1,434 finance leaders across 26 countries. Sixty percent expect AI costs and complexity to rise substantially through 2027 and say they will need more sophisticated cost-management practices. Thirty-five percent plan to keep current practices because they think the spend stays modest.
Meanwhile 43% still list embed AI to automate operations among their top three priorities. That is not a contradiction. It is a control problem.
BeSmartee’s CFO started budget season in May, not September, just to size tool cost. He put a $500 alert on Claude in Excel.
The student job is not to cheer or panic. It is to design a stage-gate, a usage ledger, and a human sign-off before the 2027 budget locks.
