Post:
The verification burden is real.

According to the Datarails 2026 CFO Sentiments Survey (early October), finance teams now spend 26% of their working week verifying or correcting AI outputs. 96% of CFOs spend at least 10% of their time on these checks.

Universal adoption has arrived. Fears of AI-driven layoffs have not materialised. What has changed is the work: less compilation, more interrogation.

Meanwhile the CFO mandate is expanding. IBM and Deloitte data show finance leaders leading AI capital allocation, cost controls, and AI trust. AI-first CFOs delivered 23% higher revenue growth — yet only 6% of organisations are transformation-ready.

On 13–14 October the AICPA issued its practice aid on AI Considerations for Financial Statement Auditors. New risks — hallucinations, model risk, explainability, bias — are now formally on the professional agenda.

For students preparing for the AI-powered economy: technical fluency is table stakes. The scarce skill is designing verification processes and exercising judgment when the model is uncertain.
