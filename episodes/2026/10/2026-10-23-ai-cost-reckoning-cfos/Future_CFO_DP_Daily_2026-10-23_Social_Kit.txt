Post:
The blank check for AI is closing.

Bloomberg reported yesterday that Wall Street banks are grappling with soaring AI bills. CFOs who approved ambitious programmes are now imposing access limits and monthly caps. One process dropped from €5 to €0.03 per run — yet at scale the absolute bill still matters. High-end models can cost the equivalent of thousands of dollars per employee per month if left unrestricted.

A SAS / FT Longitude survey released the same day: 63% of banking leaders would trade transparency for better performance. 76% expect autonomous risk decisions within five years. Only 18% currently have adequate AI governance.

Deloitte notes that 60% of finance leaders expect AI costs and complexity to rise substantially through 2027. Markets reacted on 22 October with a tech-led sell-off as concerns about AI capital spending resurfaced.

For future CFOs the work is clear: track usage and inference costs with the same rigour as headcount, install governance that moves at the speed of adoption, and make explicit sourcing decisions for every activity.
