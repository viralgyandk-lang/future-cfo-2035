Post:
On 7 October 2026 the RBI Monetary Policy Committee raised the policy repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50%. The vote on the rate was unanimous. SDF is 5.25%. MSF and the Bank Rate are 5.75%. The stance is now calibrated tightening.

Governor Sanjay Malhotra was explicit. Rate cuts are off the table in the near term. The next action can only be a hike or a pause. How far the cycle goes depends on growth, underlying inflation, whether price pressures broaden, second-round effects, and demand.

The path in is short. The easing cycle cut 125 basis points, from 6.50% to 5.25%, with the last cut in December 2025. Four meetings held. Yesterday the hold ended.

Reuters reported the inflation forecast for the year lifted to 5.2% from 5%, and the real GDP forecast to 7.1% from 6.7%. The Governor said headline CPI is expected to average almost 5.8% over the next three quarters, with core inflation at 4.4% this year.

For a CFO the useful question is not whether there will be another 50 basis points. An HDFC Bank economist has that as a view. It is not a Reserve Bank path. The useful question is whether the board pack can show, on one page, the interest delta on external-benchmark lines, the August capex note re-run at +25 / +50 / +75, and covenant headroom in basis points of EBIT.

AI can draft the table. The resolution and the bank's reset letter inform it. Finance measures the delta. Accounting checks that borrowing-cost disclosure still matches the contracts. Judgment decides whether the project was a project, or a rate view.

7 October 2026. First RBI hike in nearly four years. Unanimous 25 bps. SDF 5.25%. MSF and Bank Rate 5.75%.

A 25 bps headline is a small number. On a floating working-capital line it is not. On an August capex note signed under a neutral stance, it is a re-run, not a eulogy.

Save this for the board pack:
1. Ask the bank when the external benchmark resets.
2. Split floating, MCLR, fixed, foreign-currency.
3. Re-run capex at +25, +50, +75.
4. Know interest-cover headroom in bps of EBIT.
5. Do not book an economist's 50–75 bps path as policy.
