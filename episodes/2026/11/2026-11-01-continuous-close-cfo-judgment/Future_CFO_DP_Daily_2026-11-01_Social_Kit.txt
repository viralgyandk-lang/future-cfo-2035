Description:
How is artificial intelligence changing the close process and the role of human judgment in finance?

In this episode we examine publicly reported developments:
- Meta’s Chief Accounting Officer Aaron Anderson on moving toward continuous close while protecting SOX reliability (close reduced from 7 to 6 days with AI flux analysis).
- Deloitte surveys showing CFOs rapidly taking formal ownership of AI decisions, trust, capital allocation and cost management — while many still review costs retrospectively.
- The enduring principle: AI generates. Data informs. Finance measures. Accounting verifies. Human judgment decides.

For business students preparing for the AI-powered economy.

=== LINKEDIN ===
Hook: Meta just shortened its close by a day with AI. The harder question is who still decides what the numbers mean.

Body:
Aaron Anderson, Meta’s Chief Accounting Officer, recently described the move toward continuous close and continuous control monitoring — while insisting the team “earns the right” to give AI more autonomy only after demonstrated reliability.

At the same time, Deloitte surveys show nearly 80% of large-company CFOs now have a formal role in AI decisions, yet more than half still review AI costs after the fact. Sixty percent expect costs to rise substantially through 2027.

The practical implication for future finance leaders is clear: technology can process at scale, but human judgment remains the filter for materiality, risk and capital allocation.
